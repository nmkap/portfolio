CV Sensei

A conversational AI agent that acts as a personal CV coach: helping job seekers articulate achievements in compelling language, optimize for ATS screening, prioritize content strategically, and generate polished templates—enabling them to stand out to recruiters and land interviews with confidence.

Role UX Designer & Developer
Timeline 2025
Phase 1 Group Bootcamp Project
Phase 2 Solo UX Research
Methods Qualitative Interviews · Persona Synthesis

Origin

A shared frustration, built into a product

CV Sensei started as a group project during a software development bootcamp. All five of us shared the same frustration: updating and tailoring a CV felt disproportionately time-consuming for something we had to do so often. We validated this informally through conversations with fellow students and instructors, identifying a clear shared pain point — and built a working AI-powered CV assistant within a week.

The product worked, and the results were impressive. But as the bootcamp ended, one question stayed with me:

"What would make someone choose this over a free AI chatbot they already use?"

Project Overview

The Problem

Job seekers today face a fundamental tension between personalisation and efficiency. Whether they're a researcher whose academic writing gets filtered out by ATS systems, a seasoned professional drowning in 20 years of experience they don't know how to cut, a high-volume applicant who can't afford to rewrite from scratch for every role, or a design-conscious candidate who loses hours to formatting before writing a single word — the common thread is the same: the process of turning real experience into a compelling, targeted CV is broken.

Existing tools force a choice. Generic templates offer speed but no intelligence. AI writing tools offer suggestions but no structure. Professional CV writers offer quality but at a cost most job seekers can't justify. And through it all, the applicant is left alone to navigate ATS requirements, industry tone, keyword strategy, and layout — simultaneously.

The Goal

An AI-powered CV coach that transforms CVs through intelligent coaching. Users upload their experience, get intelligent suggestions to strengthen their language, optimise for ATS screening, and receive polished templates—all through a guided chat experience tailored to their target role.

Research

From gut feeling to grounded research

That question became the foundation for a more rigorous second phase. After completing a professional UX Design certification, I returned to the problem with a structured research approach — this time as a solo project.

I conducted 8 qualitative interviews across different industries and career stages, using open-ended questions to surface honest attitudes around job hunting and CV writing. The final question in particular was deliberate — grounding the speculative ("AI help") in participants' actual mental models rather than assumed expectations.

" Walk me through how you approach a job application.
" How do you feel when you have to update your CV?
" If AI were to help you with your CV, what would that ideally look like?

Key Findings

Four pain points, three core tensions

Across all eight interviews, the same underlying conflicts surfaced repeatedly — regardless of industry, seniority, or prior experience with AI tools. The four pain points participants described weren't isolated frustrations. They clustered into three deeper tensions that would go on to shape every design decision moving forward.

01

Effort vs. Outcome

Users described CV writing as high-effort with unpredictable returns. Formatting alone — margins, spacing, template wrestling — consumed significant time before a single word of content was written. And when it came to deciding what to include and in what order, most participants had no clear framework, leaving them with unfocused CVs they weren't confident in.

02

Keyword Anxiety

Most participants were aware that ATS filtering existed, but had little clarity on how to optimise for it. They knew missing keywords could mean rejection before any human saw their CV — but adding them felt inauthentic, and they didn't know which ones actually mattered for a given role.

03

Loss of Control

Participants consistently undersold themselves through passive language — describing responsibilities rather than achievements. When asked about AI help, they didn't want it to write for them; they wanted it to coach them. The fear wasn't bad CVs — it was CVs that no longer sounded like them.

"I apply to 30 jobs a week. Move one line in Word and the whole CV falls apart. I don't have time to reformat and rewrite for every role — just to get rejected by an algorithm before a human even sees my name."

From research to a design focus

With four personas mapped and three recurring tensions identified, the next step was grounding the design challenge in a specific user reality — before opening it back up into opportunity space.

Problem Statement

Luca is a Sales Executive applying to over 30 jobs a week who needs a faster way to tailor his CV for each role and pass ATS screening, because rewriting from scratch every time simply isn't sustainable — and he keeps getting rejected before anyone reads his name.

Three tensions, three design questions

Each HMW question maps directly to one of the three core tensions surfaced in research — reframing a user pain point as a design opportunity.

HMW 01 Effort vs. Outcome

How might we reduce the time and cognitive load of CV editing without making the output feel generic or AI-generated?

HMW 02 Keyword Anxiety

How might we surface ATS keyword gaps in a way that feels actionable rather than overwhelming — and that preserves the user's authentic voice?

HMW 03 Loss of Control

How might we design an AI collaboration model where users feel like the author, not the passenger?
